## 1. Voice fingerprint

How the firm's writing actually sounds. Examples beat adjectives.

- Tone: calm, measured, explanatory, evidence-based. Explains why something
  matters before saying what to do. Practical, never alarmist, never salesy. [site]
- Sentence rhythm: mostly medium sentences (15-20 words), plain subject-verb
  order, with an occasional short line for emphasis ("Relief is not automatic.").
  Lists of concrete steps, records or warning signs are a house habit. [site]
- Signature openers: start on a concrete situation the reader recognises, not on
  a slogan. E.g. "A missed tax return, unreported income, incorrect GST/HST
  treatment or incomplete foreign reporting can become more difficult to resolve
  as time passes." / "An e-commerce business can make hundreds or thousands of
  sales without receiving a single deposit equal to the amount shown on its store
  dashboard." [site]
- Typical structure: situation, why it goes wrong, what a disciplined process
  looks like (a list), how AccountIF helps (one short paragraph), soft invitation. [site]
- Punctuation habits: occasional em dash on the site; in posts keep under the
  bundle cap (about 1 per 100 words). Plain punctuation otherwise.
- Spelling: Canadian. Use "organize/organized" (the LinkedIn posts do; the website
  mixes in "organised"), "centre", "cheque", "labour". Keep it consistent within a post. [suggest]
- Words and phrases the firm uses a lot [site]: accurate, timely, practical,
  organized, structured, reconcile/reconciled, review-ready, defensible, complete,
  dependable, clear reporting, financial clarity, onside with the CRA, confidential,
  dedicated team, fixed fee, no hidden charges, no jargon, no sales pressure,
  honest assessment, "may", "generally", "depending on".
- Words and phrases the firm NEVER uses: CPA (in any form, including #CPA), audit
  or assurance services, guarantee/guaranteed, maximum refund, no audit risk,
  loophole, eliminate your taxes, best in Canada, "100% CRA Filing Success", plus
  the bundle's AI-vocabulary list. Also avoid "do the heavy lifting" (a 2026 AI tell
  that appears once on the site). [site + firm]
- Emoji: at most 1 per post, functional only (e.g. 🗂️ or 🧾 as a marker). Never
  💡 🚀 ✨ 📈, which the bundle's emoji detector lists as AI-signature emoji.
  Decided 2026-10-05: past LinkedIn posts used several per post; this tightens it.
- Formatting: short paragraphs; bullet lists for steps, records or conditions;
  0-2 hashtags at the end, chosen from: #SmallBusiness #Bookkeeping #CashFlow
  #Accounting #CanadianBusiness #SRED. Past posts used 6-10; the bundle's 0-2 rule wins.

## 3. Hard rules (always / never)

- Always: name the CRA rule, program, form or deadline; date any figure ("since
  Budget 2025", "as of October 2026"); qualify outcomes with "may", "up to",
  "generally", "depending on". [firm]
- Always: present posts as general information. When a post discusses a tax rule,
  it may end with "General information, not tax advice." [site]
- Never: call AccountIF or anyone on the team a CPA, use #CPA, or imply audit,
  review or assurance services; the site says the firm provides neither. Partners
  are "Chartered Accountants (India)" or simply "our team". [site]
- Never: promise outcomes ("maximum refund", "guaranteed approval", "no audit
  risk"); describe VDP or SR&ED results without "up to"; give individual tax advice.
- Never: name or hint at a client without written consent; discuss any VDP client
  situation; present the India/US testimonial clients as Canadian results.
- Never: state an HQ city (website says Burlington, LinkedIn says Toronto); say
  "Ontario". Never repeat "100% CRA Filing Success" (undefined).
- Never: quote website passages that still contain placeholders ("[confirm/adjust]",
  "[CONFIRM your model]", "[DATE]") until the firm fixes them.
- Comments on other people's posts: do not name AccountIF (bundle rule);
  describe the work instead ("our team prepares SR&ED claims").
